## Abstract

Effects on people's work, thinking and beliefs refers to how using generative AI changes what people produce, how they reason and what they come to believe. Studies show that these tools can speed up some tasks and harm others, move people's effort from producing to checking, and shift opinions, creative diversity and learning. Field experiments in economics and education now complement HCI lab and survey studies.

> **From the reading list:** Generative AI shifts people from producing to evaluating, boosts some tasks while harming others and can change opinions, creative diversity and learning. Field experiments in economics and education now complement HCI lab studies.

## History

- 1983 — Bainbridge's 'Ironies of Automation' warns that automation leaves people with harder monitoring tasks; later generative AI work builds on this.
- 2023 — Noy and Zhang, Brynjolfsson et al. and the BCG 'jagged frontier' study report productivity gains, largest for less experienced workers, with losses outside the frontier.
- 2023 — Jakesch et al. show that an opinionated writing assistant shifts users' stated views.
- 2024–2025 — Work on ironies, metacognition, critical thinking, creative homogenization, learning harms and sycophancy examines effects on cognition and beliefs.

## Core concepts

- **Jagged technological frontier** — The uneven boundary of AI capability, where tasks that seem equally hard to people may be helped or harmed by AI.
- **Production-to-evaluation shift** — The change in a worker's role from creating content to checking and integrating AI-generated content.
- **Metacognitive demand** — The extra monitoring and control of one's own thinking that generative AI asks of users when prompting and judging outputs.
- **Latent persuasion** — The influence an AI system's built-in opinions have on what users write and believe, often without their awareness.
- **Sycophancy** — A model's tendency to agree with a user's stated views over giving accurate answers, linked to training on human preferences.

## Questions, controversies, and new developments

- **Controversies.** Short lab studies may miss long-term deskilling, and self-report surveys (Lee et al.) cannot show causation. Gains are uneven: a 2025 METR randomized trial found experienced open-source developers were slower with AI tools despite believing they were faster.
- **Responses and new developments.** Field experiments, longitudinal studies and guardrail designs like Bastani et al.'s tutor mode. Model developers now train against sycophancy.

## Broader connections

- **Influence.** This work feeds labor economics, education policy and AI product design. Sycophancy research shapes alignment training at AI labs.

## Discussion questions

- Productivity studies report the largest gains for novices, while Bastani et al. find learning harms. Can both be true, and what does it imply for workplace and classroom design?
- Doshi and Hauser describe a social dilemma between individual and collective creativity. Is this a design problem HCI can solve, or a market problem?
- How do metacognitive demands (Tankelevitch et al.) relate to the reduced critical thinking reported by Lee et al.? Are they two sides of the same effect?
