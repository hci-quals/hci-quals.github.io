## Abstract

Making social activity visible means designing systems that show people what others are doing, so groups can coordinate and hold each other accountable. It matters for HCI because awareness supports collaboration without explicit coordination, while organizational incentives and privacy concerns limit what visibility can achieve.

> **From the reading list:** Awareness of others' activity lets groups coordinate and hold each other accountable, but organizational incentives can override any design.

## History

- 1992 — Dourish and Bellotti show collaborators coordinate through awareness of each other’s work in shared workspaces.
- 1992 — Orlikowski finds Lotus Notes failed at a consulting firm because incentives rewarded individual expertise.
- 2000 — Erickson and Kellogg propose social translucence: visibility, awareness and accountability.
- 2010s — Presence indicators in collaborative editors and transparency features on platforms such as GitHub apply these ideas.

## Core concepts

- **Awareness** — An understanding of others’ activities that provides context for one’s own work.
- **Social translucence** — A design approach that makes social cues visible while respecting privacy boundaries.
- **Accountability** — People’s sense that their actions are visible to and answerable to others.
- **Structuration** — The view that technology and organizational practice shape each other over time.

## Questions, controversies, and new developments

- **Controversies.** Awareness trades off against privacy and can become workplace surveillance. Orlikowski's structuration view challenges technological determinism.
- **Responses and new developments.** Work on privacy-awareness tradeoffs (Hudson and Smith, 1996) and studies of transparency on GitHub (Dabbish et al., CSCW 2012) map when visibility helps.

## Broader connections

- **Influence.** Awareness research shaped presence cursors in collaborative editors and the real-time sync algorithms (operational transforms, CRDTs) behind them. Social translucence informs open-source platform design.

## Discussion questions

- When does awareness become surveillance? Where would you draw the line in a workplace tool?
- Orlikowski’s case suggests incentives can override design. What can designers do when the organization’s rewards work against the tool?
- Erickson and Kellogg call for translucence rather than transparency. What is the difference in practice?
