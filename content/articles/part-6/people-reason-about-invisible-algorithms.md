## Abstract

This idea concerns how people notice, interpret and respond to algorithms that curate what they see, often by forming folk theories. It matters for HCI because these beliefs shape how people use and resist platforms, and because the same platform dynamics can be exploited by coordinated information operations.

> **From the reading list:** Platforms shape what people see, and users form folk theories about how. Information operations exploit platform dynamics through ordinary participation.

## History

- 2014 — Sandvig and colleagues propose auditing algorithms through sock puppets and scraping.
- 2015 — Eslami et al. find most study participants were unaware of news feed curation.
- 2017 — DeVito et al. analyze #RIPTwitter and the folk theories users held about algorithmic feeds.
- 2019 — Starbird, Arif and Wilson describe disinformation as collaborative work involving ordinary users.

## Core concepts

- **Algorithm awareness** — Whether and how users know that an algorithm is shaping what they see.
- **Folk theory** — An informal, user-built explanation of how a system works.
- **Algorithmic audit** — A study that probes a system from outside to infer how its algorithm behaves.
- **Information operation** — A coordinated effort to shape public opinion by manipulating online information.

## Questions, controversies, and new developments

- **Controversies.** Filter bubbles (Pariser) are contested by platform-run studies (Bakshy, Messing and Adamic, Science 2015), whose independence critics question. Researchers lack data access to verify platform claims.
- **Responses and new developments.** Sock-puppet and scraping audits (Sandvig et al., 2014) and the 2020 US election collaboration between Meta and academics (Guess et al., Science 2023).

## Broader connections

- **Influence.** This work started algorithmic auditing in FAccT and diversity metrics in recommender systems. It also shaped trust-and-safety engineering.

## Discussion questions

- Eslami et al. studied users in 2015. Would their findings hold today, when algorithmic feeds are widely discussed?
- Are folk theories errors to correct, or useful models that help people act?
- Starbird et al. show ordinary users take part in disinformation. How should platforms design for this without silencing participation?
