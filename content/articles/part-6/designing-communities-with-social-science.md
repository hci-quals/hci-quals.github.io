## Abstract

Designing communities with social science means using theories of motivation, coordination and governance to design features and policies for online communities. It matters for HCI because it offers testable design claims, while field results show that theory-based predictions do not always hold and that community tools can have unintended effects.

> **From the reading list:** Theories of motivation and coordination can guide community design, though predictions don't always hold. Commons governance research explains how communities sustain themselves.

## History

- 1990 — Ostrom identifies design principles for long-lasting commons institutions.
- 2004 — Beenen et al. test social-psychology-based messages to increase MovieLens contributions.
- 2008 — Kittur and Kraut show Wikipedia quality depends on coordination, not only on the number of editors.
- 2012–2013 — Kraut and Resnick compile design claims; Halfaker et al. trace Wikipedia’s editor decline to its own quality-control tools.

## Core concepts

- **Design claim** — A testable statement linking a community design choice to an expected behavioral outcome.
- **Social loafing** — The tendency to contribute less when one’s effort seems unidentifiable or unnecessary to the group.
- **Commons governance** — Rules and institutions by which a group manages a shared resource without a market or state.
- **Newcomer socialization** — The ways a community recruits, welcomes and retains new members.

## Questions, controversies, and new developments

- **Controversies.** Theory-driven designs often fail to replicate across communities. Field experiments on users raise ethics concerns, as the Facebook emotional contagion study showed (Kramer et al., PNAS 2014).
- **Responses and new developments.** Platforms and researchers now use ethics review for experiments, and Matias's CivilServant runs community-approved experiments. Halfaker et al. traced Wikipedia's decline to its own tools.

## Broader connections

- **Influence.** GroupLens's MovieLens dataset became a standard benchmark for recommender systems. This line of work also informs open-source sustainability research.

## Discussion questions

- Beenen et al. found that some theory-based predictions failed. What should designers take from partial replications?
- Halfaker et al. show protective tools drove away newcomers. How should a community weigh quality control against growth?
- Which of Ostrom’s principles map well onto online communities, and which do not?
