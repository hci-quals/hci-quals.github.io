## Abstract

Building with critical reflection is the practice of designing and building technology while questioning the hidden assumptions of one’s own technical field. In HCI it underpins reflective design and related approaches that treat systems as ways to surface and test alternatives to dominant cultural values.

> **From the reading list:** Technical fields hide their assumptions in methods and vocabulary; researchers can build systems while questioning them.

## History

- 1997 — Agre, drawing on his AI work, describes a critical technical practice with one foot in technical craft and one in reflexive critique.
- 2001 — Dourish’s Where the Action Is cites Agre’s critical technical practice as a major inspiration.
- 2005 — Sengers, Boehner, David and Kaye propose reflective design, combining cultural critique with building and evaluating systems.
- 2010s — The ideas feed critical design, speculative design and feminist and value-oriented HCI.

## Core concepts

- **Critical technical practice** — Technical work that treats its own core metaphors and methods as objects of critique.
- **Margins of a field** — Phenomena that a field’s dominant metaphors leave unexplained, which can point to alternatives.
- **Reflective design** — Design that helps designers and users reflect on the assumptions embedded in technology.
- **Unconscious values** — Cultural assumptions built into technology that neither designers nor users usually notice.

## Questions, controversies, and new developments

- **Controversies.** Critical work is accused of critiquing without building alternatives, and engineers often dismiss it. It is unclear whether reflection changes practice.
- **Responses and new developments.** Speculative, critical and adversarial design (DiSalvo, 2012) build artifacts that make critique concrete.

## Broader connections

- **Influence.** Agre is widely cited in AI ethics, and critical technical practice shaped the sociotechnical turn in FAccT.

## Discussion questions

- Agre found his critique by trying to build AI systems. Can critique emerge from building in the same way in HCI today?
- How would you evaluate a reflective design system if usability is not the main goal?
- What core metaphors in your own subfield might Agre’s method invert?
