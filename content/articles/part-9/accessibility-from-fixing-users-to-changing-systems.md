## Abstract

This idea traces accessibility research’s shift from compensating for individual deficits toward changing systems, drawing on ability-based design, disability studies and the frame of interdependence. It matters for HCI because it reframes disability as a relation between people and their environments and asks who sets the research agenda, a question sharpened by AI systems.

> **From the reading list:** Accessibility research has moved from compensating for deficits toward ability-based design, disability studies and interdependence. AI raises new questions about who it serves.

## History

- 2008 — Gajos, Wobbrock and Weld show automatically generated interfaces adapted to motor abilities improve speed and accuracy.
- 2010 — Mankoff, Hayes and Kasnitz bring disability studies and the social model into assistive technology research; VizWiz uses crowds to answer blind users’ visual questions.
- 2011 — Wobbrock et al. publish ability-based design principles in TACCESS.
- 2018–2023 — Bennett et al. propose interdependence; Mack et al. survey the field; Gadiraju et al. study disability bias in LLMs.

## Core concepts

- **Medical vs. social model** — Disability as individual impairment to fix versus as a product of environmental and social barriers.
- **Ability-based design** — Designing systems that adapt to users’ abilities instead of requiring users to adapt.
- **Interdependence** — A frame that sees access as achieved through relationships among people and technologies, not independence alone.
- **Human-powered access** — Using remote or crowd workers, as in VizWiz, to provide access in near real time.

## Questions, controversies, and new developments

- **Controversies.** Medical versus social models of disability; Liz Jackson's “disability dongles” name flashy solutions disabled people did not ask for. Disability simulation exercises are criticized as misleading (Bennett and Rosner, CHI 2019).
- **Responses and new developments.** Participatory work led by disabled researchers, ability-based design and interdependence framing.

## Broader connections

- **Influence.** Accessibility research shaped OS accessibility APIs and screen readers. Vision-language models now power assistive apps such as Be My AI, reviving VizWiz's goals with new risks.

## Discussion questions

- Is ability-based design compatible with the social model, or does it still locate the problem in individual users?
- VizWiz depends on crowd workers. How does the interdependence frame change how we evaluate that design?
- Gadiraju et al. found subtle rather than overtly offensive harms. What evaluation methods would catch those?
