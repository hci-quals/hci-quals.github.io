## Abstract

HCI became a discipline in the early 1980s by joining cognitive psychology with computer engineering to study and design the user interface. Its histories explain why the field mixes science, engineering and design, and why its object of study keeps moving outward from hardware to groups and organizations.

> **From the reading list:** HCI formed by fusing cognitive psychology with engineering, and its object of study has kept moving outward. These readings explain why the field looks the way it does.

## History

- 1970s — Xerox PARC brings psychologists and engineers together; Card and Moran study text editing and pointing.
- 1983 — The first CHI conference is held; Norman presents design principles drawn from cognitive science.
- 1990 — Grudin describes the interface moving outward from hardware to software, cognition, and social groups.
- 1998–2017 — Myers credits university research for key interface advances; Grudin's book traces HCI across several fields.

## Core concepts

- **Applied information-processing psychology** — Using models of perception, memory and motor control to predict how people will use an interface.
- **The interface moving outward** — Grudin's claim that the focus of interface design shifted from hardware to software, cognition, and then groups and organizations.
- **Hard science vs. design** — The long-running dispute over whether HCI should produce predictive models or support practical design.
- **Research-to-product lag** — The often long gap between a university or lab invention and its commercial adoption, which Myers documents.

## Questions, controversies, and new developments

- **Controversies.** Is HCI a science, engineering or design? Newell and Card (1985) argued for hard psychological science; Carroll and Campbell's “Softening Up Hard Science” (1986) replied that such models miss real design. Grudin shows human factors, information systems and CHI never fully merged.
- **Responses and new developments.** The field became openly pluralist (Part X, paradigms), without settling the question. AI now pulls HCI's center of gravity again, as Grudin's later book notes.

## Broader connections

- **Influence.** HCI became a recognized CS subfield with SIGCHI. Usability engineering entered software engineering practice, and UX became a profession.

## Discussion questions

- Grudin's 'computer reaches out' story ends with groups. Where is the interface reaching now, and does AI fit the same pattern?
- Is HCI a science, an engineering field or a design field? Which reading makes the strongest case, and what does each answer exclude?
- Myers emphasizes university research and Card and Moran emphasize industrial labs. What changes when we credit one or the other?
