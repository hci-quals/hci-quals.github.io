## Abstract

Embodied cognition is the view that thinking depends on the body and physical action, not only on internal mental processes. For HCI it suggests that interfaces using gesture, movement and physical objects can support reasoning, learning and collaboration, though the strength of the evidence varies by claim.

> **From the reading list:** Physical action supports reasoning, learning and collaboration. Embodied cognition in psychology supplies both the evidence and the cautions.

## History

- 1990s — Situated and embodied views of cognition gain ground in psychology and HCI.
- 2001 — Dourish’s Where the Action Is frames embodied interaction for HCI.
- 2002 — Wilson separates six distinct claims made under the banner of embodied cognition.
- 2006 — Klemmer, Hartmann and Takayama propose five themes for how bodies matter in design.

## Core concepts

- **Embodied cognition** — The view that cognitive processes are shaped by the body and its interaction with the world.
- **Epistemic action** — Physical action taken to make thinking easier rather than to change the world directly.
- **Offloading** — Using the environment to hold information or do cognitive work.
- **Thinking through doing** — Klemmer et al.’s theme that physical activity and gesture support reasoning and learning.

## Questions, controversies, and new developments

- **Controversies.** Several high-profile embodiment findings, such as power posing, failed to replicate. Strong embodiment (cognition is bodily) and weak versions (bodies sometimes help) are often conflated.
- **Responses and new developments.** Wilson's taxonomy separates the claims. Research has focused on robust effects, like gesture aiding learning (Goldin-Meadow's work).

## Broader connections

- **Influence.** Embodiment research informs embodied AI in robotics, VR avatar design (the Proteus effect, Yee and Bailenson, 2007) and the learning sciences.

## Discussion questions

- Which of Wilson’s six views does Klemmer et al.’s argument rely on most?
- Given failed replications of some embodiment findings, which design claims are on solid ground?
- Do touchscreens and VR count as embodied interaction in Klemmer et al.’s sense?
