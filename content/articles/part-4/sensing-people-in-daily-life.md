## Abstract

Everyday sensing uses sensors on the body and in the environment to support interaction and behavior change in daily life. It enables personal informatics and new input methods, and raises questions about privacy and about how to evaluate long-term effects.

> **From the reading list:** On-body and in-environment sensing enables new interaction and behavior-change systems, but it raises questions about how to evaluate long-term effects.

## History

- 2008 — Consolvo et al. field-test UbiFit Garden with automatic activity inference and an ambient phone display.
- 2010 — Li, Dey and Forlizzi propose a stage-based model of personal informatics.
- 2011 — Harrison, Benko and Wilson’s OmniTouch turns everyday surfaces into touch input with a shoulder-worn depth camera.
- 2011 — Klasnja, Consolvo and Pratt argue HCI should evaluate behavior-change technologies differently from clinical trials.

## Core concepts

- **Personal informatics** — Systems that help people collect and reflect on information about themselves.
- **Glanceable display** — A display that conveys status at a glance, such as UbiFit’s garden wallpaper.
- **Stage-based model** — Li et al.’s five stages of preparation, collection, integration, reflection and action.
- **Behavior change evaluation** — Klasnja et al.’s argument that HCI studies should test whether designs work as intended rather than prove lasting behavior change.

## Questions, controversies, and new developments

- **Controversies.** Engagement with self-tracking fades and can cause anxiety, and health data raises privacy concerns. Clinical researchers see small, short HCI studies as weak evidence.
- **Responses and new developments.** Lived informatics (Epstein et al.) describes lapsing and resuming tracking. Micro-randomized trials and just-in-time adaptive interventions (Klasnja, Nahum-Shani and colleagues) bring experimental rigor.

## Broader connections

- **Influence.** This work underlies mobile health and wearables, activity-recognition ML and digital phenotyping in psychiatry.

## Discussion questions

- Klasnja et al. argue HCI need not show long-term behavior change. Do you agree, and what should count as evidence?
- Where does Li et al.’s stage model break down for people who lapse and resume tracking?
- OmniTouch and UbiFit sense very different things. What privacy questions does each raise?
