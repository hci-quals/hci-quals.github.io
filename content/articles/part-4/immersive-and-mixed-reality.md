## Abstract

Immersive and mixed reality systems place users in or alongside computer-generated environments, usually through head-mounted displays. Their research questions center on presence, the sense of being in the virtual place, and on where a system sits between fully real and fully virtual.

> **From the reading list:** VR and AR began with one head-mounted display; their research questions now center on presence and on where along the real–virtual continuum a system sits.

## History

- 1968 — Sutherland demonstrates a tracked, see-through head-mounted display with wireframe graphics.
- 1994 — Milgram and Kishino define the reality–virtuality continuum and a taxonomy of mixed reality displays.
- 2009 — Slater separates place illusion from plausibility as components of realistic response in VR.
- 2010s — Consumer headsets make VR and AR common research platforms.

## Core concepts

- **Reality–virtuality continuum** — A spectrum from real environments through augmented reality and augmented virtuality to fully virtual ones.
- **Presence** — The subjective sense of being in a virtual environment.
- **Place illusion** — Slater’s term for the feeling of being in the place shown, despite knowing one is not.
- **Plausibility illusion** — The sense that events in the virtual environment are really happening.
- **Cybersickness** — Nausea and discomfort caused by immersive displays.

## Questions, controversies, and new developments

- **Controversies.** Presence has no agreed definition or measure; Slater publicly rejected Witmer and Singer's questionnaire (1999). Cybersickness affects many users, with reported gender differences.
- **Responses and new developments.** Physiological and behavioral measures of presence, Slater's place-illusion framework and rendering and locomotion techniques that reduce sickness.

## Broader connections

- **Influence.** VR drives research in graphics, tracking and rendering, and it is used for robot teleoperation, simulation training and exposure therapy.

## Discussion questions

- Sutherland’s display was see-through. Was the first head-mounted display AR or VR on Milgram and Kishino’s continuum?
- Can presence be measured well with questionnaires, or does it require behavioral measures as Slater suggests?
- Does the continuum still describe today’s passthrough headsets well?
