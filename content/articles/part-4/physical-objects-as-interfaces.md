## Abstract

Tangible user interfaces give digital information a physical form that people can grasp and manipulate. Later work on shape-changing displays aims for materials that can change form under computer control, extending HCI beyond screens.

> **From the reading list:** Tangible and shape-changing interfaces give digital information physical form, so people can grasp and manipulate it.

## History

- 1995 — Fitzmaurice, Ishii and Buxton’s Bricks introduce graspable user interfaces.
- 1997 — Ishii and Ullmer present Tangible Bits with interactive surfaces, graspable objects and ambient media.
- 2012 — Ishii et al. propose Radical Atoms, a vision of dynamic, transformable physical materials.
- 2013 — Follmer et al. build inFORM, a pin-based shape display that renders and actuates physical forms.

## Core concepts

- **Tangible user interface** — An interface where physical objects both represent and control digital information.
- **Ambient media** — Background displays such as light, sound or airflow that convey information peripherally.
- **Shape display** — A surface, often an array of actuated pins, that can change its physical shape.
- **Dynamic affordances** — Physical cues for action that a system can change over time.

## Questions, controversies, and new developments

- **Controversies.** Tangible systems are costly, hard to scale and rarely leave the lab. Claims that they improve learning outrun the evidence (Marshall, 2007).
- **Responses and new developments.** Learning studies show mixed results. Researchers turned to digital fabrication and data physicalization (Jansen et al., CHI 2015).

## Broader connections

- **Influence.** Tangible work connects to actuation and haptics research in robotics and to digital fabrication. It also shaped educational kits such as tangible programming blocks.

## Discussion questions

- Tangible systems rarely leave the lab. Is that a failure of the idea or of the technology?
- What does inFORM let people do that a touchscreen with haptic feedback could not?
- Radical Atoms is a vision paper. How should a reading group evaluate a vision that cannot yet be built?
