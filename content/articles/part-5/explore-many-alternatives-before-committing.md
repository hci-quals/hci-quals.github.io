## Abstract

Exploring many alternatives means generating and comparing several design options, through sketches and prototypes, before committing to one. It matters for HCI because both experiments and design theory suggest that comparison improves the final design and the feedback received, while fixation on early ideas or examples narrows what designers consider.

> **From the reading list:** Prototypes are questions, sketches are explorations, and comparing several options yields better designs and feedback. Fixation research explains why people fail to do this.

## History

- 1991 — Jansson and Smith show that engineers copy features of example solutions, demonstrating design fixation.
- 1997 — Houde and Hill classify prototypes by what they explore: role, look and feel, or implementation.
- 2006–2007 — Tohidi et al. show multiple designs draw more critical feedback; Buxton distinguishes exploratory sketches from prototypes.
- 2008–2010 — Lim et al. describe prototypes as filters; Dow et al. show parallel prototyping beats serial iteration.

## Core concepts

- **Design fixation** — Blind adherence to an existing idea or example that limits the range of solutions considered.
- **Prototype as question** — Treating each prototype as a tool for answering a specific design question.
- **Sketch versus prototype** — Sketches suggest and explore possibilities; prototypes test and confirm specific ideas.
- **Parallel prototyping** — Creating several alternative prototypes at once and getting feedback on them together.
- **Filter** — A prototype’s focus on some qualities of a design while leaving others out.

## Questions, controversies, and new developments

- **Controversies.** Fixation effects vary with expertise and example type, and parallel prototyping costs time teams may not have. Showing alternatives may increase critique without improving the final design.
- **Responses and new developments.** Example-gallery tools and studies of AI-induced fixation (e.g., Wadinambiarachchi et al., CHI 2024) test these effects with generative tools.

## Broader connections

- **Influence.** Design Galleries (Marks et al., SIGGRAPH 1997) brought alternative exploration to graphics. The idea also runs through A/B testing culture and generative design in engineering.

## Discussion questions

- If parallel prototyping produces better results, why do teams still commit early? What costs do the Dow et al. experiments leave out?
- Does generative AI that produces many alternatives on demand reduce fixation or create a new form of it?
- How do Houde and Hill’s and Lim et al.’s frameworks differ in what they say a prototype is for?
