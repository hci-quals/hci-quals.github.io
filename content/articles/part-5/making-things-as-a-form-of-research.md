## Abstract

Research through design is the view that making artifacts can itself be a way of producing research knowledge, not just a step before evaluation. It matters for HCI because it gives design work a recognized place in a field built on scientific and engineering norms, while raising questions about how such knowledge is judged and how it generalizes.

> **From the reading list:** Research through design argues artifacts can carry knowledge, but the field debates what standards should judge it and how it generalizes.

## History

- 2003 — Fallman argues design is central to HCI research and distinguishes conservative, romantic and pragmatic accounts of design.
- 2007 — Zimmerman, Forlizzi and Evenson propose research through design with four lenses for judging contributions.
- 2012 — Gaver argues RtD theory is provisional and grounded in examples and should not be held to scientific standards.
- 2012 — Höök and Löwgren propose strong concepts as intermediate-level knowledge between single artifacts and general theory.

## Core concepts

- **Research through design (RtD)** — A research approach in which designing and building artifacts is the main means of producing new knowledge.
- **Intermediate-level knowledge** — Design knowledge more general than one artifact but less general than a theory.
- **Strong concept** — A generative design idea about interactive behavior that applies across several designs.
- **Annotated portfolio** — A collection of design examples annotated to show the ideas and qualities they share.

## Questions, controversies, and new developments

- **Controversies.** Reviewers question RtD's rigor and generalizability and what counts as its knowledge contribution. Gaver argues imposing scientific standards destroys what makes it valuable.
- **Responses and new developments.** Strong concepts and annotated portfolios (Gaver and Bowers, 2012) offer intermediate forms of generalizable design knowledge.

## Broader connections

- **Influence.** RtD has little reach in the rest of CS. Its influence runs mainly through design schools and speculative or critical design (Dunne and Raby).

## Discussion questions

- Zimmerman et al. and Gaver disagree on whether RtD should meet shared evaluation criteria. Which position would better serve a CHI reviewer, and which would better serve designers?
- Is a strong concept a contribution that the rest of computer science would recognize? What would make RtD more legible outside design communities?
- Pick a recent CHI design paper. What knowledge does the artifact carry that the paper’s text does not?
