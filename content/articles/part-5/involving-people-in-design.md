## Abstract

Involving people in design covers approaches in which future users take part in shaping technology, as co-designers in participatory design or as sources of inspiration through probes. It matters for HCI because it raises questions of who holds power in design, whose needs are served, and how methods change when moved to new settings.

> **From the reading list:** Users can be co-designers, sources of inspiration, or hosts for probes. Later work asks who benefits and how communities are treated.

## History

- 1970s–1980s — Scandinavian participatory design projects involve trade unions and workers in designing workplace systems.
- 1998–1999 — Kensing and Blomberg review PD; Gaver, Dunne and Pacenti introduce cultural probes for open-ended inspiration.
- 2003–2007 — Hutchinson et al. define technology probes; Boehner et al. show HCI turned probes into data collection.
- 2019 — Harrington, Erete and Piper show community design workshops can reproduce inequity and propose changes.

## Core concepts

- **Participatory design** — An approach in which the people who will use a system take part in designing it, with attention to power and politics.
- **Cultural probe** — A packet of open-ended materials given to participants to provoke inspirational responses rather than collect data.
- **Technology probe** — A simple, adaptable working technology deployed in a real setting to learn about needs and inspire design.
- **Tokenism** — Participation that appears inclusive but gives participants little real influence over outcomes.

## Questions, controversies, and new developments

- **Controversies.** Participation can be tokenistic or extractive, and Scandinavian PD lost its labor politics when exported. Boehner et al. show probes were turned into data collection.
- **Responses and new developments.** Design justice, community-based participatory research from public health and critiques like Harrington et al. push toward shared power and benefit.

## Broader connections

- **Influence.** PD shaped user involvement in agile and requirements engineering and civic tech. It now informs participatory AI (Birhane et al., 2022).

## Discussion questions

- Boehner et al. argue that probes lost their epistemology when HCI adopted them. Has the same thing happened to participatory design?
- What would it take for a design workshop with an underserved community to benefit the community as much as the researchers?
- Cultural probes and technology probes share a name. How do their goals and assumptions differ?
