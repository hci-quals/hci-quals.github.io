## Abstract

The computers-as-social-actors (CASA) finding is that people automatically apply social rules and expectations to machines that show even minimal social cues. Cognitive science explains this through anthropomorphism and intuitive psychology, and the finding matters more as systems converse with their users.

> **From the reading list:** People automatically apply social rules to machines that show minimal social cues. Cognitive science explains this as anthropomorphism and intuitive psychology, which matters more now that systems talk back.

## History

- 1944 — Heider and Simmel show people describe moving geometric shapes in terms of goals and personalities.
- 1994 — Nass, Steuer and Tauber show people are polite to computers and apply other social rules to them.
- 2007–2016 — Epley et al. give a three-factor theory of anthropomorphism; Jara-Ettinger et al. model mind-reading as a utility calculus.
- 2020 — Gambino, Fox and Ratan propose machine-specific social scripts to update CASA.

## Core concepts

- **CASA paradigm** — Testing whether a known social-psychology effect holds when one partner is replaced by a computer.
- **Mindlessness** — Nass's explanation that people apply social scripts automatically, without believing the computer is a person.
- **Anthropomorphism** — Attributing human-like mental states to nonhuman agents, driven in Epley et al.'s account by knowledge, motivation for understanding, and social connection.
- **Naïve utility calculus** — The idea that people infer others' goals by assuming they act to maximize rewards minus costs.
- **Machine-specific scripts** — Gambino et al.'s proposal that people develop social scripts tailored to machines rather than copying human ones.

## Questions, controversies, and new developments

- **Controversies.** It is unclear whether CASA effects survive as novelty fades, and some replications are weaker. Anthropomorphism may be mindless error or a reasonable stance (Dennett's intentional stance), and designing to exploit it raises ethical concerns for companion bots.
- **Responses and new developments.** Gambino et al. propose machine-specific social scripts. NLP researchers now study anthropomorphism's harms directly (Abercrombie et al., “Mirages,” EMNLP 2023).

## Broader connections

- **Influence.** CASA founded research on conversational agents and shaped human-robot interaction. Inverse-planning models of goal inference (Baker, Saxe and Tenenbaum, 2009) are used in robotics.

## Discussion questions

- CASA studies used plain text interfaces in the 1990s. Should we expect the effects to grow or shrink with fluent conversational AI?
- Is anthropomorphism a mindless error or a reasonable stance toward systems that act on goals? What does each view imply for design?
- When is it ethical to design a system to trigger social responses, and who decides?
