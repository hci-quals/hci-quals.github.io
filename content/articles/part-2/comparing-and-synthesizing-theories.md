## Abstract

Comparing and synthesizing theories is the work of setting HCI's many borrowed frameworks side by side to see what each explains and misses. Because no single theory covers the field, these comparisons help researchers choose a lens and make the field's theoretical commitments explicit.

> **From the reading list:** No single theory covers HCI, so the field needs ways to compare what each one sees and misses. These readings are the maps.

## History

- 1992 — Carroll and Rosson describe the task-artifact cycle and claims analysis for scenario-based design.
- 1996 — Nardi compares activity theory, situated action and distributed cognition.
- 2004–2006 — Rogers surveys new theoretical approaches and their limited use in design; Kaptelinin and Nardi develop activity theory for HCI.
- 2017 — Hornbæk and Oulasvirta compare seven concepts of what interaction is.

## Core concepts

- **Activity theory** — A framework from Soviet psychology that analyzes human activity as subject, object and mediating tools within a community.
- **Task-artifact cycle** — The pattern in which new artifacts change the tasks people do, which in turn create needs for new artifacts.
- **Claims analysis** — Recording the upsides and downsides of a design feature as testable claims tied to scenarios.
- **Unit of analysis** — What a theory treats as the thing to study: an individual, an activity, a situation, or a sociotechnical system.
- **Concepts of interaction** — Hornbæk and Oulasvirta's set of views of interaction, e.g. as dialogue, transmission, control, experience or embodied action.

## Questions, controversies, and new developments

- **Controversies.** HCI theory is criticized as borrowed and rarely tested, and Rogers found designers seldom use it. Liu et al. (CHI 2014) mapped two decades of CHI keywords and found no stable intellectual core.
- **Responses and new developments.** Responses include the push for generative theories (Part III) and Oulasvirta and Hornbæk's problem-solving framing (Part X).

## Broader connections

- **Influence.** Activity theory spread to information systems and software engineering. Scenario-based design and claims analysis influenced requirements engineering.

## Discussion questions

- Rogers found designers seldom use HCI theory. Is that a problem with the theories, with how they are taught, or with what designers need?
- Pick one recent CHI paper. Which of Hornbæk and Oulasvirta's concepts of interaction does it assume, and would its conclusions change under another?
- Nardi compared three theories by unit of analysis and treatment of time. What would you add as comparison dimensions today?
