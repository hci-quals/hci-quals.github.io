## Abstract

This idea asks who visualizations are designed for and who is left out, including people with disabilities and communities affected by data. It matters for HCI because visualization is now common in public life, and accessibility and power shape who can understand and benefit from data.

> **From the reading list:** Visualization reflects who collects and presents data, and it excludes readers who cannot see it unless designers provide alternatives.

## History

- 2010s — Research grows on screen-reader access to charts, alt text and sonification.
- 2020 — D'Ignazio and Klein's Data Feminism argues data science should examine and challenge power.
- 2022 — Lundgard and Satyanarayan's four-level model guides what chart descriptions should convey.

## Core concepts

- **Semantic content levels** — Descriptions ranging from chart construction, to statistics, to trends, to domain context.
- **Blind and low-vision readers** — Users whose access to charts depends on text, sound or touch.
- **Data feminism** — An approach to data science informed by intersectional feminism and attention to power.
- **Examine power** — Asking who benefits from and who is harmed by a data system.

## Questions, controversies, and new developments

- **Controversies.** Critics call data feminism's challenge to objectivity political; supporters reply that neutrality claims are political too. It is unsettled whether AI-generated chart descriptions serve blind readers.
- **Responses and new developments.** Accessible visualization toolkits, sonification and description models like Lundgard and Satyanarayan's.

## Broader connections

- **Influence.** These works shape accessibility in scientific publishing, ML chart captioning and data ethics courses.

## Discussion questions

- Lundgard and Satyanarayan found blind and sighted readers preferred different levels of description. What should designers do with that?
- How does Data Feminism's critique of power apply to the visualization tools and research in this part?
- Is accessibility best handled as an add-on (alt text) or built into how visualizations are specified?
