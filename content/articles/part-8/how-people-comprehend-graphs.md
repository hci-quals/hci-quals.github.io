## Abstract

Graph comprehension research asks what cognitive processes people use to read a graph and draw conclusions from it. It complements perceptual studies by covering knowledge, expectations and decision-making, which matter when visualizations are used to inform real choices.

> **From the reading list:** Reading a chart is a cognitive process of matching visual patterns to mental schemas and questions. Cognitive science models this process directly.

## History

- 1990 — Pinker models graph reading as matching a visual description to a graph schema.
- 1990s–2000s — Studies by Shah, Hegarty and others show prior knowledge and graph format shape interpretation.
- 2018 — Padilla et al. propose a dual-process framework for decision making with visualizations across fields.

## Core concepts

- **Graph schema** — Knowledge of how a type of graph maps visual features to data.
- **Visual description** — The mental representation of a graph's visual features built during perception.
- **Dual-process account** — Decisions arise from both fast intuitive (Type 1) and slower deliberate (Type 2) processes.
- **Cognitive fit** — Performance improves when the visualization's format matches the task's demands.

## Questions, controversies, and new developments

- **Controversies.** Graph comprehension models are not yet predictive enough to guide design, and expertise effects are large. How to measure visualization literacy is unsettled.
- **Responses and new developments.** The VLAT literacy test (Lee, Kim and Kwon, 2017) and decision-focused frameworks like Padilla et al.'s.

## Broader connections

- **Influence.** These ideas inform statistics education and chart accessibility. Chart question-answering benchmarks in ML test similar comprehension skills.

## Discussion questions
