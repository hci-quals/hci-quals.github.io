## Abstract

The idea that every method trades something off holds that no research strategy can simultaneously maximize generality over populations, precision of measurement and realism of context. For HCI, a field that mixes experiments, field studies and system building, it explains why methods should be chosen to fit the problem and why a single study cannot settle a question.

> **From the reading list:** No method maximizes generality, precision and realism at once, so researchers choose by the problem they are solving.

## History

- 1980s — McGrath’s research strategy circumplex arranges methods by their trade-offs among generality, precision and realism.
- 1995 — “Methodology Matters” is reprinted in Readings in Human-Computer Interaction, bringing it to HCI audiences.
- 1997 — Stokes’ Pasteur’s Quadrant challenges the basic/applied split with use-inspired basic research.
- 2016 — Oulasvirta and Hornbæk frame HCI as problem-solving; Wobbrock and Kientz catalog seven contribution types.

## Core concepts

- **Three-horned dilemma** — McGrath’s claim that generality, precision and realism cannot all be maximized together.
- **Research strategy circumplex** — A map of eight strategies, from lab experiments to field studies, positioned by their trade-offs.
- **Pasteur’s quadrant** — Research that seeks fundamental understanding while also being inspired by use.
- **Problem-solving capacity** — Judging research by how much it improves the ability to solve empirical, conceptual or constructive problems.
- **Contribution types** — Empirical, artifact, methodological, theoretical, dataset, survey and opinion contributions.

## Questions, controversies, and new developments

- **Controversies.** HCI argues over whether it is a science at all and whether reviewers value contribution types equally; systems and design papers often feel judged by empirical standards.
- **Responses and new developments.** Contribution-type frameworks (Wobbrock and Kientz) and CHI's revised reviewing criteria name different kinds of contribution explicitly.

## Broader connections

- **Influence.** McGrath's framework was adapted for software engineering research methods (Stol and Fitzgerald's ABC framework, 2018).

## Discussion questions

- Where on McGrath’s circumplex does a typical CHI systems paper sit, and what does it give up?
- Is most HCI research in Pasteur’s quadrant, or in Edison’s (pure applied)?
- Does Oulasvirta and Hornbæk’s problem-solving view risk downplaying validity concerns?
