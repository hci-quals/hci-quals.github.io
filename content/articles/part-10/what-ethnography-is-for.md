## Abstract

This idea holds that ethnography’s value in HCI lies in understanding people and practices, not in producing a list of design implications. It matters because it shapes how fieldwork is reviewed and used, and it allows for the conclusion that the right response may be not to build technology at all.

> **From the reading list:** Ethnography's value is understanding, not a list of design implications, and sometimes the right answer is not to build.

## History

- 1994 — Shapiro’s “The Limits of Ethnography” questions how ethnography and other social sciences should feed into CSCW design.
- 2006 — Dourish’s “Implications for Design” argues the demand for design sections narrows ethnography’s contribution.
- 2011 — Baumer and Silberman describe cases where the implication is not to design technology.

## Core concepts

- **Implications for design** — The conventional closing section translating fieldwork into design recommendations.
- **Ethnography as analysis** — Ethnography as a way of interpreting social life, not just a data-gathering method.
- **Not designing** — Recognizing that technology may not be the appropriate response to a problem.
- **Interdisciplinary translation** — How findings from one discipline become usable in another.

## Questions, controversies, and new developments

- **Controversies.** Should ethnography produce design implications? Crabtree et al.'s “Ethnography Considered Harmful” (CHI 2009) criticized new “ethnographies” that abandoned the method's analytic commitments.
- **Responses and new developments.** Dourish's argument shifted expectations, and design-oriented fieldwork methods like contextual inquiry offer a middle path.

## Broader connections

- **Influence.** Ethnography shaped requirements engineering, industry UX research and STS collaborations.

## Discussion questions

- If ethnography should not end in design implications, how should HCI reviewers judge its contribution?
- Shapiro wrote before Dourish. What problems did they share, and where do they differ?
- What evidence would justify concluding “do not design technology” in a paper?
