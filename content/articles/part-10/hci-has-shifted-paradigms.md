## Abstract

This idea holds that HCI has moved through distinct paradigms, from human factors to information-processing cognitivism to a situated, meaning-centered view of interaction. It matters because each paradigm defines what counts as a good question and good evidence, which explains disputes over methods and evaluation in the field.

> **From the reading list:** The field moved from human factors to cognitivism to situated meaning, and each wave needs its own methods and keeps lessons from the last.

## History

- 1980s — Human factors and cognitive models (e.g., GOMS) dominate, treating the user as an information processor.
- 1990s — Second-wave work emphasizes groups, workplaces, participatory design and situated action.
- 2006 — Bødker describes third-wave challenges: home, culture, experience and multiple contexts.
- 2007 — Harrison, Tatar and Sengers present the three paradigms at alt.chi, arguing the third requires different validity criteria.

## Core concepts

- **Paradigm** — A shared set of questions, methods and standards of evidence, after Kuhn.
- **Human factors paradigm** — Optimizing fit between human and machine, with errors as problems to remove.
- **Classical cognitivism** — Treating interaction as information flow between human and computer minds.
- **Phenomenologically situated paradigm** — Interaction as meaning-making that is embodied and embedded in context.

## Questions, controversies, and new developments

- **Controversies.** The “waves” story oversimplifies and implies first-wave human factors work is obsolete, though it continues. Some see the third paradigm as rejecting scientific HCI.
- **Responses and new developments.** Later proposals, such as Frauenberger's “Entanglement HCI” (TOCHI 2019), describe a fourth wave grounded in posthumanist theory.

## Broader connections

- **Influence.** The paradigm debate is mostly internal to HCI, but it shapes how HCI explains itself to the rest of CS.

## Discussion questions

- Is “paradigm” the right word, given that all three approaches coexist in today’s CHI?
- Bødker warns against discarding second-wave lessons. Which ones does current HCI risk losing?
- Where does research on AI and LLM interaction fit, and does it suggest a fourth wave?
