## Abstract

This idea holds that controlled user studies are often the wrong test for systems, toolkits and social computing research, and that evaluation should match the type of contribution. It also notes that participants’ responses are biased by their relationship to the researchers. It matters because review norms shape which kinds of systems HCI can publish.

> **From the reading list:** User studies are often the wrong test for systems, toolkits and social computing; evaluation should fit the contribution, and participants' responses are biased by who built the system.

## History

- 2007 — Olsen proposes criteria for UI systems research, warning of a “usability trap.”
- 2008 — Greenberg and Buxton argue usability evaluation can be harmful when applied at the wrong time.
- 2011–2012 — Bernstein et al. critique evaluation norms for social computing; Dell et al. show participants favor the researcher’s artifact.
- 2018 — Ledo et al. categorize evaluation strategies for HCI toolkits.

## Core concepts

- **Usability trap** — Olsen’s warning that simple usability tests favor easy, low-impact systems.
- **Evaluation fit** — Choosing evaluation methods that match the claims a contribution makes.
- **Toolkit evaluation strategies** — Ledo et al.’s categories: demonstration, usage, technical performance and heuristics.
- **Participant response bias** — Participants’ tendency to favor the system they believe the researcher made.

## Questions, controversies, and new developments

- **Controversies.** Reviewers demand user studies even when they don't fit, novelty is overvalued and participant response bias inflates positive results.
- **Responses and new developments.** Olsen's criteria, Ledo et al.'s evaluation strategies and Bernstein et al.'s reviewing advice give alternatives.

## Broader connections

- **Influence.** These arguments parallel debates in ML about benchmark-only evaluation and in systems research about artifact evaluation.

## Discussion questions

- What would convince you a toolkit is valuable if it has no user study?
- Is Greenberg and Buxton’s critique still relevant now that many papers include field deployments?
- How should Dell et al.’s findings change study designs in HCI4D and elsewhere?
