## Main idea

To design better collaborative AI, we need data on the writing *process* (interactions), not just the final writing outputs. If we want a principled way to say what language models are good and bad at, we should collect interaction data on writing and analyze it. Writing is treated as an interactive process in which the user specifies intent, the model produces output, and the human steers and refines it.

## What is the paper trying to do?

Build a dataset for studying language-model capabilities in collaborative writing. The researchers collected granular interaction data — cursor movement, text typed — and mapped it onto semantic tasks such as navigating, asking for an AI suggestion, or rejecting a suggestion.

## What kind of contribution is it?

An instantiation of the idea readings as a dataset and an analytic lens: a way to study model capabilities through situated writing, rather than from outputs alone.

## Why does it matter?

It connects directly to symbiosis: symbiosis requires an emphasis on interaction or activity, not on output or artifact. HCI research is interested in interaction, and the data it analyzes should reflect that. The qualities of agents as collaborators cannot be understood unless they are studied in situated interactions.

## Hot takes / discussion

The sharpest distinction is *interaction traces versus outputs*.

An assumption sits next to a doubt. The assumption is that human intelligence can be understood by recording and studying observable interaction patterns. The doubt: would we get better data about the user’s actual mental model if we measured cognitive or neuroscientific activity, or found a reliable way to ask for reflections? Bush’s EEG technology might finally come in handy. Can human intelligence and activity be meaningfully understood from external patterns of behavior, or is internal state (for example EEG) necessary?
